# 07. Reference analysis: Publicis Sapient and Scaler

Prepared by TechnoTaau Team, brand strategist, 4 September 2026, for Jakhar
Singh. Input to Phase 2 copy and design; does not change the v4 base or any
Gate 1 decision on its own. Proposed edits to the brief and spec are listed
in section 9 for the Design Lead to accept or reject.

## 1. What this answers

The client re-sent two references today with three instructions: use them
for design and content ideas, choose the best for the homepage from the
course documents and the existing site, and make the page do one thing in
their words: the user stays, reads, gets confident, and fills the lead form
or contacts us. They also want the page to feel like a top-class premium
institution with legacy experience, and to avoid the phrases that most AI
course marketing uses.

Method: each homepage was fetched as text on 4 September 2026, plus the
Publicis Sapient customer stories and company pages and the Scaler Academy
program page. The Scaler FDE program URL returned 404. Text fetches reveal
structure, copy and the presence of media, not the exact look, so visual
notes below are limited to what the markup shows. Everything is then held
against `docs/00-brief.md` section 5b, `docs/01-brand.md` and the verified
section of `docs/02-content-inventory.md`.

The short version: Publicis Sapient is the right reference for how a senior
firm talks about proof and legacy, and its cheapest ideas are all writing
decisions. Scaler is the right reference for how a program row answers a
professional's questions, and almost everything else on it is either scale
TEKMentors cannot back or bootcamp tactics the brief rules out.

## 2. Publicis Sapient (publicissapient.com)

### First screen

A thin ticker above the header rotates four announcements with award icons,
two of them product awards, one a product ranking, one a case number ("3M
lines of COBOL to clear specs in 8 weeks"). Header: Solutions, Industries,
AI Platforms, Customers, Resources, Company, Careers, search, and one button,
"Let's Connect". Hero headline: "We Build AI Solutions for the Enterprise".
Subhead: "Publicis Sapient brings the experts who understand your business
and the platforms to modernize systems, scale AI agents and keep operations
resilient." Directly under it, "Trusted by leading organizations in every
industry" and a wall of nineteen named logos (Walmart, Marriott, Lufthansa,
Nestlé, Nissan, Chevron, Verizon, Heathrow, Audi and others).

The first screen makes one claim, names who backs it, and offers one door.
Nothing on it asks for anything.

A note for the client: the live tekmentors.com hero, "We build
enterprise-grade AI solutions", is almost word for word the Publicis Sapient
line. A CTO who knows both sites will read that as imitation from a firm of
2 to 10 people. The v4 headline is already better and this memo recommends
moving further away, not closer.

### Section order

1. Ticker of awards and one case number
2. Hero: claim, subhead, one primary CTA
3. Client logo wall, named
4. "We understand and solve your enterprise issues": four named barriers
   (legacy technology, disconnected work, reactive operations, missing
   context)
5. "Where enterprise expertise meets AI": people ("30+ years of enterprise
   transformation experience") and platforms
6. Three platform sections, each with a plain mechanism paragraph and
   "Learn more"
7. "Results from our customers' transformations": three case cards, each
   with a client label, a summary and two or three metrics
8. "We're purpose-built for enterprise AI": four counters (30+ years,
   20,000 people, 26+ countries, 3 platforms)
9. "Industry recognition": three awards
10. "Trending topics": three dated articles, "Perspectives from the
    practitioners solving these challenges for our customers"
11. "Ready to learn more?": a four-line agenda for the conversation and a
    six-field form with a marketing consent checkbox
12. Nine-column footer

The rhythm is claim, proof of who trusts us, the problem in your words, the
mechanism, the results, the scale, the thinking, the door. Problem before
mechanism, mechanism before numbers.

### How it builds confidence

- Named logos immediately under the hero, no captions, no counts.
- Case cards that label the client by sector and scale when the name is
  withheld: "Global Retail and Commercial Bank", "a Global CPG Leader".
  Named when allowed: "Nissan Cuts IT Costs 40%". The label does the trust
  work that a logo would.
- Numbers presented as pairs or with a baseline: "nearly 3 million legacy
  COBOL lines into audit-ready specifications in 8 weeks", "62% same-day
  issue resolution", "99.9% platform uptime maintained".
- Legacy stated in a sentence, not only a counter: "For more than 30 years,
  enterprises have used our technology to solve their hardest problems."
  The company page adds a dated timeline from 1990 and a quote from the CEO
  by name.
- Third-party recognition (awards, a Hugging Face leaderboard position) in
  its own section, kept apart from the case numbers.
- Faculty do not appear; this is a services firm. People are argued as
  "experts who understand your business" and the practitioners behind the
  articles.

### How it keeps people reading

Density is low per section and every section has one job. Headings are
declaratives that name a stake: "The gap is never the model" would sit
comfortably among them. Case headlines lead with the outcome and the client
type. Article titles are opinions with dates: "No AI ROI? Start With
Understanding How Work Really Happens", "AI Adoption Is Up. Enterprise
Impact Still Lags". Each platform paragraph explains a mechanism in one
breath ("It reads the code you already have, recovers the business rules
buried inside it and rebuilds on top of them so nothing critical gets lost
along the way"). The reader is never asked to admire; they are told what
happens.

### CTA strategy and lead path

One button in the header, "Let's Connect", and one form at the bottom.
Everything else is "Learn more" or "Explore". The form is preceded by an
agenda: "Discuss your unique business challenges", "Explore AI solutions
built for your industry", "Get proven delivery guidance", "Identify the next
step that fits your goals". Six fields including country, plus a marketing
opt-in checkbox. The lead path is one conversation with a person, which is
the same promise v4 makes with fewer fields and no opt-in.

### Tone of voice, three quoted lines

- "We understand and solve your enterprise issues"
- "It works on top of the tools you already use, so reliability goes up
  while the cost of running IT comes down."
- "Perspectives from the practitioners solving these challenges for our
  customers."

The usable register is the plain mechanism sentence and the outcome-first
case headline. The slogans are not usable: "While others talk, we deliver",
"Purpose-Built for Enterprise AI", "next-generation AI technology", and the
case title "AI-Powered Personalization at Scale" all use phrases on the
TEKMentors banned list. Publicis Sapient also uses title case throughout,
which the client has already rejected.

### Visual system as far as text reveals it

Award icons in a ticker, a logo wall, industry icons in the navigation, a
timeline carousel of "Moments of Impact" on the company page, a "Short Doc"
video series on the customer stories page, partner logos (Adobe, AWS, Google
Cloud, Microsoft, Salesforce). Motion and video are structural, as the
reconciliation memo already noted.

### Budget-tied versus free

Tied to budget TEKMentors does not have: the video documentary series,
photography, the animated ticker, custom platform illustrations, the
"Moments" carousel, a nine-column footer that needs a large site behind it.

Costs nothing, because it is a writing or structure decision: sector and
scale client labels; outcome-first case headlines; metrics shown as
before-and-after pairs; legacy stated in prose with a date; an agenda for
the first conversation next to the form; dated, opinionated article titles
with a practitioner byline; one header button and one form.

## 3. Scaler (scaler.com)

### First screen

Header in capitals: PROGRAM, MASTERCLASS, AI LABS, ALUMNI, RESOURCES,
Placement Report. Hero: "The market has already changed", then "Become the
Professional Built for the Next Decade in AI", then "Strong technical
foundations, AI integrated at every stage, and a curriculum that evolves as
the market does". A program list follows (five programs, one flagged
"Introducing a new in-demand role: Forward Deployed Engineer") with
"Discover the program". Two CTAs: "Placement Report", "Request A Callback".
A sticky bottom bar carries a phone number and "Request a Call" on every
page.

The first screen sells urgency and a callback. It is built for paid traffic
that already knows it wants a course.

### Section order

1. Hero with program list and callback CTA
2. "Built Different, Designed to Last": four pillars (AI-integrated
   curriculum, AI-powered platform, lifelong access, strong foundations)
3. "AI-first curriculum built by 100+ engineers from" plus a logo marquee,
   then a brochure download CTA
4. "Find the AI Path for your role": tabbed program cards. Each card: title,
   rating with count ("4.8+", "25K+ Ratings"), duration, three numbered
   "What you'll build" lines, a "role evolution" line, "Go To Program" and
   "download brochure"
5. "Real AI-era career moves, not generic success stories": alumni cards
   with before and after employer, number of offers, salary hike, a quote,
   video thumbnails
6. "The People Behind Scaler": a marquee of instructor photos
7. Podcast: four episodes with named founders and CTOs
8. "This isn't an AI content library. It's a system built around AI-era
   progress": six pillars with imagery
9. "shaping careers since 2019": four outcome counters (career transition
   rate, median CTC, median hike, top-quartile CTC) and "Placement Report"
10. Blog: four articles with author and read time
11. "Featured in the News": five dated press items
12. "Frontier AI relevance": enterprise delivery logos, institutional
    partnership logos (IIT Roorkee, IIM Trichy, Woolf), a government program
13. Footer: legal name, registered address, ISO 27001 badge, app QR code,
    long SEO link lists, sticky callback bar

Sixteen sections and a CTA in nearly every one. It is a funnel page, not an
institution page.

### How it builds confidence

Volume. Ratings with counts, named alumni with before and after employers
and offer counts, video testimonials, instructor cards with employer, years
and one distinguishing fact ("Two-time ACM ICPC World Finalist", "Built
Facebook Messenger", "750+ live sessions"), a per-instructor rating, a
placement report, dated press coverage from named outlets, institutional
partnerships with logos, ISO certification, market statistics on the program
page ("163% growth in AI-related job postings", "$206K average AI engineer
salary"), and full fee transparency (₹3,99,000, ₹9,791 a month, ₹20,000
upfront) with "Next cohort starts Aug 2026".

Two observations matter for TEKMentors. First, the outcome counters in
section 9 arrived in the fetched HTML as "0%", "0LPA", "₹0 LPA": they are
animated counters that only fill with JavaScript. Any crawler, any reader
with scripts blocked, and any screen reader sees zeros. That is exactly the
"0+" failure on the live tekmentors.com, and it is a reason to keep the
v4 rule that numbers are static text. Second, the instructor card format
(institution or employer, years, one specific fact) is the only proof
element on the page that TEKMentors can fill honestly today, and it can be
filled from the client's own flyer.

### How it keeps people reading

High density, marquees, carousels, tabs and video. Headings are punchy and
argumentative: "Real AI-era career moves, not generic success stories",
"In the AI era, proof matters, more than empty promises", "The program ends.
The access does not". The "What you'll build" triplets are the single best
piece of structure: three concrete things per program, numbered, each one a
deliverable rather than a topic. The canvas already borrowed this and the
spec keeps it.

### CTA strategy and lead path

"Request a Callback" in the hero, mid-page, in a modal, and in a sticky
bottom bar with a phone number. "Download Brochure" on every program card.
"Placement Report" as a secondary CTA everywhere. The lead path is a phone
call from a sales team, which is the bootcamp pattern. TEKMentors' promise
is a written reply from a consultant, and the reconciliation memo already
chose that; nothing here should change it.

### Tone of voice, three quoted lines

- "Become the Professional Built for the Next Decade in AI"
- "You're not buying a 2026 snapshot. You're buying a living system. Built
  to last"
- "Software Engineering hasn't changed. What it takes to be great at it,
  has."

The third line is good writing and shows that Scaler can be direct. The rest
is the register the brief calls the AI-flyer genre: "Built for the Next
Decade", "AI-Powered Platform", "Master communication through discovery
calls, design reviews, executive briefings, incident war rooms", "Not caught
up. Ahead of it". Section 5b names Scaler as not a tone reference, and the
fetch confirms why.

One more overlap to watch. Scaler's FDE program description ("discovery
calls, design reviews, executive briefings") is close to the v4 FDE row
("discovery calls, design reviews, and briefing an executive who has eight
minutes"). Both derive from the same job description, but TEKMentors' row
should lean on what Scaler's homepage does not show: the flyer's eight named
phases and the named stack (FastAPI, LangGraph, MCP, pgvector, OpenSearch,
Terraform, OpenTelemetry). Specific tools are institutional; job-description
verbs are generic.

### Visual system as far as text reveals it

SVG and WebP throughout, instructor photo marquee, alumni video thumbnails,
podcast thumbnails, module preview graphics, badges (ISO 27001,
certificate mockup), a company logo marquee, tabbed and carousel components,
capitalised navigation, animated counters. The page depends on photography
of real students and instructors and on video.

### Budget-tied versus free

Tied to budget: video testimonials, instructor and student photography, a
podcast, in-house platform screenshots, animated counters, marquees,
carousels, a placement report (which also needs a cohort and an assessment
methodology).

Costs nothing: the numbered "What you'll build" triplet; the instructor
credential line format; "Next cohort starts" as a line in the program row;
fee and instalment shown in the row once the client supplies them; a clear
"who this is for" line per program; FAQ per program page.

## 5. Do not pull

- Scale counters: "30+ years, 20,000 people, 26+ countries" and "1,00,000+
  alumni, 37,000+ community". TEKMentors is 2 to 10 people. Any counter row
  echoes the live site's "0+" and the banned canvas counters. Scaler's own
  counters rendered as zeros in the fetch.
- Awards ticker and "Industry recognition". No verified award exists; "10+
  awards won" is already on the do-not-publish list.
- Ratings with counts ("4.8+", "25K+ Ratings"). No basis, and the format is
  bootcamp.
- Alumni before-and-after cards with offer counts, hike percentages,
  placement reports, median CTC, third-party salary statistics. Salary
  ranges and placement claims are on the do-not-publish list.
- Market statistics ("163% growth in AI job postings", "729% growth in FDE
  roles"). Unverifiable on the page, and this is the AI-flyer genre the
  client wants to leave behind.
- Institutional partnership logos (IIT Roorkee, IIM Trichy, Woolf). TEKMentors
  has IIT (BHU) alumni on the faculty. That is a credential of people, not a
  partnership with the institute, and the copy must never let a reader
  confuse the two.
- Marquees, carousels, video testimonials, a podcast, animated counters,
  scroll reveals. The brand doc allows one hero animation; the canvas dropped
  the slider for a reason recorded in the export README.
- Publicis Sapient's slogans and title case: "While others talk, we
  deliver", "Purpose-Built for Enterprise AI", "AI-Powered", "next-generation".
  Scaler's "Become the Professional Built for the Next Decade in AI", "Master
  communication", "Not caught up. Ahead of it". All banned or genre.
- The current tekmentors.com hero line "We build enterprise-grade AI
  solutions", which is the Publicis Sapient hero with one word moved. Retire
  it on every page, not just the homepage.
- A six-field form with country and a marketing opt-in (Publicis Sapient),
  or a callback-first funnel with a phone modal on every screen (Scaler).
  Four fields, a consultant's reply, no newsletter: the v4 form stays.
- A "Moments of Impact" timeline. Only the founding year is dated in the
  verified inventory. Revisit if the client dates the UK bank, financial
  institution and Kenya engagements in writing.
- Footer link farms. The footer stays four columns.

## 6. Dwell-time and confidence strategy

A senior visitor keeps scrolling when each screen answers the question the
previous one raised, and stops when a screen asks them to admire something.
So the page should move in the Publicis Sapient order: a claim they can
check, the problem in their own words, the mechanism, the numbers, the
people, the door. v4 already has that spine. What the references add is
precision in the joints.

The first screen has to carry the legacy in one quotable sentence: since
2017, IIT (BHU) faculty, twenty-plus years each, clients typed by sector.
The routing card then makes the visitor pick their own problem, which is the
first act of staying. The consulting diagram and the engagement phases show
the mechanism without adjectives. The case card converts belief into a
number with a baseline, which is the point where a CTO decides the firm is
real. The mentor bench then answers who will be in the room, in the same
format a Scaler visitor is used to but without the ratings. The straight
answers remove the six objections before they are typed.

Trust enough to write comes from three things this page can do and a
bootcamp cannot: name the people, show one signed number with its baseline,
and say exactly what happens after the form. Nothing on the page should
count, rotate, or shout. Restraint is the evidence of seniority; the numbers
are the evidence of competence.

## 7. Positioning line candidates

Each pair is a hero headline and a one-line proposition for the subhead.
Only facts from the verified section of the content inventory are used:
founded 2017, Gurugram, IIT (BHU) faculty, 20-plus-year careers, a large UK
bank, an international financial institution, a multinational consultancy
serving Fortune 500 clients, and the four UK bank numbers. No superlatives,
no flyer phrases, sentence case, no em dashes.

1. Headline: "Twenty years each in enterprise engineering. Now teaching
   production AI."
   Proposition: "TEKMentors, Gurugram, since 2017. Consulting for a large UK
   bank, an international financial institution and a multinational
   consultancy serving Fortune 500 clients. AI programs taught live by
   IIT (BHU) engineers with 20-plus years each."

2. Headline: "We took a UK bank from six-month releases to 90 days. We teach
   engineering the same way."
   Proposition: "Practitioner-led AI programs and enterprise consulting from
   Gurugram, founded 2017, faculty from IIT (BHU) with 20-plus-year careers."

3. Headline: "Enterprise engineering since 2017. AI programs taught by the
   consultants who still do the work."
   Proposition: "IIT (BHU) faculty, 20-plus years each, with a client record
   that includes a large UK bank and a multinational consultancy serving
   Fortune 500 clients. Live online, on site in Delhi NCR, or hybrid."

4. Headline (v4, kept): "We build the AI systems, and the people who keep
   them running."
   Proposition (new subhead): "Since 2017, from Gurugram: DevOps, agile and
   cloud transformation for banks and financial institutions, and AI
   engineering programs taught by IIT (BHU) engineers with 20-plus years
   each."

5. Headline: "Production AI, taught by people a UK bank trusted with its
   release pipeline."
   Proposition: "TEKMentors, founded 2017. IIT (BHU) faculty with 20-plus
   year careers. Annual releases from 10 to 15, cycle time from six months to
   90 days, defects from 25% to 10%, on one engagement we can show you."

Recommendation: candidate 1. It puts the three institutional facts the
client named into the first two sentences, it is quotable, and it does not
depend on the "same people" inference that candidates 2, 3 and 5 rest on
(see the claims check). Candidate 4 is the fallback if the client wants to
keep the reviewed v4 headline: the new subhead alone moves the legacy into
the first screen. Candidate 2 is the strongest for audience B alone and the
weakest for a graduate reading it on a phone.
