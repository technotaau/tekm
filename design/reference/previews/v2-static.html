01 · QUESTIONS

Six questions we are asked in almost every first meeting.

Answered the way we would answer them across a table, without the hedging. If your version of the question is missing, it is probably the interesting one, so send it to us.

01

Where should we actually start with AI?

With the process your best people complain about most. It is usually somewhere unglamorous, has a number attached to it already, and nobody has offered to fix it because it is boring. That is exactly why it is the right first project.

02

Our pilot works. Why can it not go live?

Because a pilot proves the model and production needs everything else: data access with permissions attached, an evaluation set, a deployment path, monitoring, and a named owner on Monday morning. We build those in from week one, which is slower for a fortnight and faster by a quarter.

03

Do we hire AI engineers or train ours?

Train the ones who already know your systems, your data and who to call when something breaks at nine on a Friday. That knowledge takes years to rebuild. The AI part takes weeks, and that is the part we teach.

04

How do we know it is not quietly getting worse?

You build the evaluation set before you build the feature, and you run it on every change. Without it, quality is a matter of opinion, and the loudest opinion in the room wins. With it, a regression shows up before your customers find it.

05

We are agile on paper. Why is delivery still slow?

Because the ceremonies were adopted and the decision rights were not. Teams cannot go faster than the approvals above them. We coach the managers and the governance alongside the teams, which is the unpopular half of agile work and the half that moves the numbers.

06

What happens when you leave?

Your engineers keep running it, because they built most of it with us watching rather than the other way round. Handover is a phase in the plan with a date on it, not a conversation we have when the invoice is due.
